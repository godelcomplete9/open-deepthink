FORMAL FRAGMENT: POSITIONAL NUMERAL SYSTEMS

Definition (positional value). A numeral system with radix b expresses a
natural number v as the finite sum

    v = Σ_k d_k · b^k,        d_k ∈ {0, 1, ..., b − 1}

The coefficient d_k is the k-th digit; the digit constraint d_k < b closes
the expansion and the carry rule wraps digits at b^k.

Binary radix. For b = 2 the alphabet is {0, 1} and the digit string of v is
its bit expansion. One byte is an 8-bit window: values are taken modulo
2^8 = 256, so the byte ground space has exactly 256 equivalence classes.

Decimal radix. For b = 10 the alphabet is {0, ..., 9} and the digit string
is the familiar decimal expansion of v.

Equivalence statement (radix homomorphism). Positional encoding is a ring
homomorphism from (v, +, ×) to digit vectors with carry: addition of values
corresponds to digit-wise addition followed by carry normalization. The
radix parameter b is the only datum needed to regenerate every digit
instance from the single value statement.

Modulus. The byte modulus 256 acts as the closing constraint of the ground
scale: instances 0..255 are distinct classes, and any value wraps via mod
256. Hexadecimal radix 16 is the same construction on the alphabet
{0,...,9,a,...,f}.
